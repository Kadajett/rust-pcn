## The real words matter—and do not contradict the argmax statistics

**User-provided measurement, accepted as reported:** the 28 post-objective prose answers contain 616 characters, 36 distinct characters, 78 distinct word-like tokens, and **50 dictionary words among 97 tokens of length at least two**. Examples include “The tore cand in the pore/ s for” and “The formed cantis buly.” These figures were not recomputed here.

The two measurements ask different questions:

1. **Teacher-forced held-out argmax:** choose one highest-scoring byte at each of 512 fixed, true-text contexts, without generation penalties. Seventeen winning byte IDs says nothing about how many other bytes have meaningful second-, third-, or fifth-place scores.
2. **Free generation:** feed each chosen byte back into the next context. These contexts differ from the held-out corpus and from one another. The aggregate spans many generated positions and evolving checkpoints.
3. **The decoder modifies the ranking.** `generation.rs::generate_text_with_policy` applies four-byte n-gram blocking and the presence penalty, **plus** `repetition_adjusted_score`’s immediate-repeat and recent-count penalties (`318–332, 496–559`). This can select bytes that never win an unmodified held-out argmax. The inspected runtime policy has `sampling: null`: variety does not require random sampling. Importantly, `TextDecodePolicy::GREEDY` disables the newer policy penalties but still runs the older repetition adjustment; a future genuinely raw-greedy control must bypass both.

**Inference:** recognizable, recurring words and word fragments are positive evidence that the learned scores contain useful short-range language structure. The decoder does not contain a dictionary that inserts “formed” or “there.” It can nevertheless manufacture diversity, and common short words are obtainable from simple character n-grams. Consequently, the reported word rate does **not yet distinguish** learned whole-word representations from character-level regularities, prove progress beyond a matched bigram/trigram generator, or establish that answers address their prompts. “It has learned some spelling/local word structure, but not demonstrated reliable answering” is the fair description.

**Better future comparison:** identical saved weights and prompts, raw-greedy versus current decoding, alongside train-only character n-gram controls with the same decoding constraints. Report dictionary rate together with word-length distribution, longer-word accuracy, prompt relevance, and exact expected-versus-actual answers. Keep decoding policy fixed across training comparisons. Top-5, target margins, frequency-stratified ranks, and eventually properly defined byte NLL can reveal progress hidden by top-1; **current mean rank is not evidence of overnight improvement**, because it actually deteriorated. ## A in detail: vocabulary is a representation choice, not the missing switch

**Literature:** Chang et al. trained five 124M GPT-2-style models with **50K SentencePiece tokens**. At step100, **99.8%** of sampled outputs were “the,” comma, or period. Their predictions subsequently peaked in resemblance to unigram, bigram, and higher-order n-gram models, in order. Thus a large vocabulary does not prevent the frequent-token phase. Their example coherent sentence appears after hundreds of millions of supervised tokens, not a universal number of optimizer steps transferable to River. [L1]

**Literature:** ByT5 and autoregressive MEGABYTE demonstrate useful text generation from small byte alphabets. They establish that bytes are expressive enough, not that River’s particular PC objective is adequate. MEGABYTE changes the architecture to handle long byte sequences efficiently; ByT5 reports sequence-length/compute tradeoffs. [L2, L3]

BPE/subwords can genuinely help efficiency: fewer prediction steps per word, more raw text per fixed token-context length, and reusable lexical units. It is **not only a relabeling of units**. But it also makes targets sparser, enlarges the output distribution, changes input representations and training data, and invalidates direct comparisons of byte top-1/perplexity with token top-1/perplexity. Compare log probability per byte on identical raw text under an explicit tokenization/evaluation convention, not raw percentages. None of this diagnoses vocabulary size as River’s bottleneck.

**Source correction about the 4,097 columns:** `universal.rs:53–55` reserves that many token-support coordinates, but `universal_corpus.rs:930–945` rejects targets above byte EOS256 and supervises only the first **257**; `universal.rs::InheritedByteScorer::scores` copies only **257** scores (`1053–1063`). They are **not an existing trained 4,097-token BPE vocabulary**. The request expert is also deliberately untrained in this prose-focused run. Merely using the reservation would not create a tokenizer, token labels, lexical embeddings, or a trained decoder; switching to it would discard the advantage of the trained shared byte path.

**Calculated lower-bound memory costs, FP32 and hidden width9,216:** expanding the active byte head while preserving all other columns requires `9,216 × (V−257)` additional weights. Replacing each of the 16 recent-byte one-hot slots by a V-way token slot adds `16 × 9,216 × (V−257)` first-layer weights.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 4,097 | 35,389,440 | 135 MiB | 2.109 GiB |

These exclude biases, batch states, cached activities/errors, weight-update temporaries, and optimizer moments. Checkpoint storage doubles corresponding weight additions if both expert shapes grow. Reusing already reserved output coordinates avoids that particular output allocation but changes their contract; it does not avoid the input/learning/migration problem. Sparse inputs alone do not eliminate a dense first-layer weight matrix. Learned compact token embeddings could avoid the 16×one-hot explosion, but that is a new locally trained representation and checkpoint migration, not a flag. On a **6GB GPU**, the 32K one-hot input is ruled out by weights alone; even the 4K option is material, while output expansion alone is **not** proven to exceed available memory. No live GPU-memory measurement or vocabulary experiment was performed. Width expansion is outside this assignment’s constraints anyway.

**Verdict A [Inference; moderate confidence]:** retain bytes for the immediate repair investigation, while keeping representation efficiency as a live alternative explanation. Tao’s controlled vocabulary gain and CANINE/Charformer’s naive-character failures argue against an absolute “vocabulary cannot help” position. They do not demonstrate a feasible, checkpoint-preserving conversion that fixes these outputs within two hours. Revisit tokenization with that evidence and migration contract—not because twenty argmax byte types means the model cannot spell more words. [T1–T5]

Different batches and training stages are mixed; this is a trajectory warning, not a same-data causal loss comparison. Current byte spectral magnitude was around **1.8**, below cap10, rank-one share around **3.4%**, with zero reported cap hits. This is not evidence that a spectral emergency caused those rollbacks.

**Source:** both phases use the same implemented energy, including the conditional term, but separate fresh initialization and finite relaxation (`gpu/mod.rs::settle_masked_phases`, `767–860`). The energy guard adds steps only above its numerical energy ceiling, not when a terminal equilibrium residual is too large.

**Inference:** if free and hard-clamped phases globally minimized the same energy over nested feasible sets, `min(E_free) ≤ min(E_positive)`. Their finite100-step results need not obey that inequality: incomplete settling or different local basins suffice. A negative gap is **not by itself proof of a sign error, irreparable collapse, or mandatory rollback**. Its persistent appearance warrants checking the terminal forces and contrasting update components. Lower total reconstruction energy does not establish better byte prediction. The historical “7ppm clamp signal” diagnosis and the old recommendation to warm-start only the positive phase must not be copied forward as if the conditional change and subsequent collapse investigation never happened. Keep the fresh-phase safeguard. [P4]

### 2. Eta is not the effective learning rate of every block

**Source:** `multimodal.rs::multimodal_output_update_scale` sets byte-column scale32. The inherited batch loop (`train_universal.rs::run`, `3602–3614`) multiplies it by `batch_rows / byte_head_reference_batch_size`, currently128/192. The weight updater (`gpu/tensors.rs::update_weights_gpu_contrastive`, `540–608`) multiplies the averaged contrastive correlations by eta and SEAL modulation, then by the output scale. The byte bias receives that output scale too; the lower generative biases do not.

For a full batch, the byte multiplier is **32×128/192 = 21.333**, versus trunk scale1. Ignoring SEAL and the differing correlation magnitudes, byte averaged-update coefficients are therefore **0.064**, **0.021333**, and **0.007111** at eta0.003,0.001,0.000333 respectively. These are **coefficients, not measured relative parameter changes**. A single larger eta accelerates an already-faster tied byte block and bias along with any weak trunk signal.

### 3. What the recorded rollbacks actually show

**Measured from `health.json`:**

- **October4, 10:04:29 PM PDT:** b5003 → savedb4811; eta0.003 →0.001. Three top-1 readings **19.92%,18.55%,19.73%**, each below0.8×25.59% reference.
- **October5, 3:28:05 AM PDT:** b7581 → savedb7196; eta0.001 →0.00033333336. Readings **16.02%,13.09%,9.77%**, each below0.8×21.19% reference. At the final failed evaluation, **top-5=34.57% and mean rank109.85**, not just one unlucky argmax change.

The first event followed an **objective change at the already configured eta**, not a clean controlled eta-only increase. The second developed at a constant eta0.001. Therefore “raising eta caused it” is plausible but incomplete; objective mismatch, slowly changing inference conditioning, and data ordering remain competing explanations. The reduced rate has restored the approximate top-1 level; it has not removed the upward conditional residual trend.

**Literature/inference:** ordinary LMs manage large update transients with warmup, schedules, adaptive per-parameter steps, norm clipping, normalization/initialization, and sometimes weight averaging. Warmup softens a transition; it cannot make an unsafe final rate safe. Clipping limits update size, not directional bias. Adam rescales supplied local derivatives; it does not require backpropagation, but cannot turn an incorrect contrastive estimator into a correct one. Averaging can reduce trajectory variance, not repair a systematically wrong objective. [L5–L8, P1, P5]

### 4. Normal early learning and actual representation limits coexist

**Inference:** the real-word observation and non-space hits favor some lexical learning; incomplete source exposure leaves further learning plausible. Neither proves continued progress in prompt-conditioned answering, where the complete observed record remains0/47. Input visibility also weakens subject truncation as the common explanation for these particular short failures. However:

- **Source:** the inherited scorer keeps the last64 context bytes, with recent16 one-hot coordinates, and zeros the existing full-request/full-state conditioning rows (`universal.rs::byte_scoring_input`, `1007–1026`; `CONDITION_INPUT_ROWS`, `28–30`). Prepared inherited responses copy the same limited prefix (`universal_corpus.rs::prepared_response_byte_example`). Once an identical generated suffix displaces the question, different questions become indistinguishable. More examples cannot recover information absent from the input.
- **Source:** the registry appends datasets in sequence, and the trainer processes raw corpus, Pinball rehearsal, shared response rows, then focus rows in blocks (`dataset_registry.rs::load_registry_stage`, `604–745`; `train_universal.rs:3591–3604`). Response positions are also emitted in ascending order within records. This is a plausible non-i.i.d. update/forgetting problem, **not yet a demonstrated cause of the plateau**.
- **Not established:** current post-objective W1/W2 update magnitudes, saturation, phase convergence, a probability-calibrated loss curve, or matched train-set memorization. These missing measurements prevent a claim that River is simply waiting for grokking—or that it cannot improve without a particular proposed fix.

### Numerical context for the retained first-pass works

**Literature, not River measurements:** [L1] used five124M models,1M steps,batch256×128; step100≈3.3M supervised targets,step10K≈330M. Frequent-first text is not an architecture-independent clock. [L2] ByT5 uses256bytes+specials and reports≈33% pretraining wall-time overhead versus its token counterpart. [L3] MEGABYTE’s PG19 word-normalized perplexity36.4 versus compute-matched byteTransformer69.4 is meaningful; the separately published subwordTransformer-XL36.3 is **not** a same-compute controlled comparator. [L4] grokking examples can fit training data before1000steps and generalize around1M; some operations still do not generalize with95% of the data. These are small algorithmic tables, not natural-language underfitting.

**Literature:** [L5] Post-LN’s output-side gradient scale explains warmup sensitivity; Pre-LN changes that condition. [L6]160M/1B LM experiments find small-batch momentumSGD competitive, while large-batchSGD can diverge without clipping and still trailAdam with clipping;5–20% warmup does not erase the phenomenon. [L7] a1.7B baseline diverges at learning rate.001 despite5% warmup, while the proposed initialization/embedding scaling stabilizes it; Pascanu’s clipping paper supplies the dynamical foundation, not a PC-specific cure. [L8]125M GPT2 at70Ksteps reports loss2.963 original,2.949EMA,2.917LAWA; one tuned averaging result is not a reason to blend incompatible rollback trajectories.

**Literature:** [L9] studies34 attention models with causal induction-head evidence strongest in small attention-only models, not an inference that River contains those circuits. [L10] studies0.5B/1B curriculum models with early/mid gains that can fade or reverse late. [L11]66M PTBLSTM perplexity78.4 baseline,76.6 uniform smoothing,76.3 unigram smoothing,74.7 confidence penalty concerns regularized categorical models, not proof that smoothing repairs a weak Gaussian PC objective.

**Literature:** [P1] uses AdamW weights/SGD activities in many experiments, but includes adverse width scaling; its32–4096width study is shallow vision, not a trained9K-wide River prescription. Its MSE/CE reversals are in the counter-evidence table. [P2] conditionalLM uses one attention block/head,width128,context32,8001BPE symbols,200Ktrain/10Kdev examples,10seeds; BP162.64±.76, generalizedPC175.90±1.74, GaussianPC590.08±12.60 test perplexity, with best5inference iterations. [P3] μPC reaches128-layer residual fully connected vision nets using coordinated forward/inference/weight scaling; the setup uses batch64,Adam weights,GD activities and no biases. **Literature:** [P4] equilibrium-propagation guarantees require equilibria and a small continuous nudge; finite fresh hard-clamped phases do not automatically meet those hypotheses. [P5] Adam Algorithm1 maintains first/second moments of whatever derivatives are supplied; it does not prove those derivatives optimize the intended task. [P6] iPC’s LM has two attention blocks,one head,width128,8001BPE symbols,200Ktrain/10Kdev examples; conditional test perplexityBP113.32,PC206.34,iPC142.54, but only7/10iPC runs pass the convergence filter versus10/10others. ## Ranked checkpoint-preserving fixes and two-hour falsification criteria

**All five below are inference/proposals, not implemented or deployed.** This is a conservative **investigation order, not a proven causal ranking**: confidence that any one remedy will improve generated answers within two hours is low. Each preserves accumulated weights, the shared prose/code path, hidden width, byte alphabet and100 relaxation steps. Every remedy must earn its place through **Expected vs actual text**, not through its diagnostic metric. A later authorized cutover must resume the then-latest exact state, preserve its recovery anchor/data cursors, and keep health gates active; it must not reset the run. Do not implement all five at once. Times below begin after verified implementation and a durable-boundary cutover, not at the start of engineering.

### 1. Make the existing 100-step inference better conditioned; establish whether the contrastive direction is usable

- **Map:** `gpu/mod.rs::settle_masked_phases`, `GpuPcn::settle_step`; `gpu/tensors.rs::euler_layers_gpu`, `relax_byte_prediction_gpu`, `update_weights_gpu_contrastive`; `train_universal.rs::GeneratorTelemetry` and `portal/app.js::renderGenerator`.
- **Change:** report terminal **unclamped activity-force RMS** per layer/phase, energy change near steps90–100, positive/free state separation, and positive/free local-update cancellation. Then use a bounded positive diagonal/block preconditioner of the **complete local activity force**, including the byte term, where measured conditioning warrants it. This changes how the same energy is settled, not the stored weights or the iteration budget. Reuse the current settling entry points across training, heldout and generation. Existing control: `--inherited-layer-alphas`; there is **no inspected trainer CLI for the proposed general diagonal preconditioner**. The existing `GpuPcn::set_top_relaxation` conditioned solver is reusable machinery to study, not proof it solves this broader issue or a currently available deployment flag.
- **What the user should see [Inference]:** if finite settling is blocking credit, later answers should contain intact, task-relevant words instead of malformed common-opening strings—for example an answer naming the asked-for planet, not another “The tore …” variant. Improved force residuals without that textual change are only a diagnostic result. Preconditioning preserves ideal stationary points, but finite100-step decoded text may change immediately even before learning.
- **Risk:** extra reductions/workspace; a diagonal approximation may not resolve coupled modes. Smaller alpha can reduce oscillation **or** worsen under-settling. Do not choose it merely because it is smaller; do not restore the superseded warm-start-only-positive behavior.
- **Timing and rejection [Inference]:** an immediate post-cutover answer change could be altered inference, not learning; look for sustained prompt-fit improvement across the next approximately7–8 dashboard answers in two elapsed hours. Lower terminal-force RMS and less late-step drift support the mechanism. If forces were already small, if only the energy-gap sign improves, or if text stays equally irrelevant, this has not broken the user-visible plateau. Rosenbaum’s early-layer mismatch after>100 iterations and Laborieux’s estimator-bias result are strong reasons this fix can fail even with excellent settling. [P1, P3, P4, O1–O3]

### 2. Separate trunk versus byte/bias step sizes, with transition warmup and norm-limited local updates

- **Map:** `multimodal.rs::multimodal_output_update_scale`; the inherited batch loop in `train_universal.rs::run`; `gpu/tensors.rs::update_weights_gpu_contrastive`; `train_universal.rs::roll_back_to_last_healthy` and checkpoint metadata in `src/universal_checkpoint.rs`.
- **Existing smallest ablation:** at the effective eta0.00033333336, changing **`--byte-head-reference-batch-size 192 → 384`** halves the full-batch byte/bias coefficient to **0.003556**, leaving the trunk coefficient unchanged. This is a falsifiable conservative starting trial, **not an established optimal value** and not an instruction executed here. Keep precision1 and the100-step profile fixed for attribution.
- **Next implementation if warranted:** independently schedule block steps and clip the **final local update norm after SEAL/output scaling**, with a relative norm cap and an absolute floor for near-zero biases. Warm up only the changed step over a bounded transition, e.g.128 inherited updates; preserve the ledger’s safety ceiling. New block schedules/clipping would require explicit new controls and exact checkpoint/rollback state, not hidden constants.
- **Adaptive option after direction checks:** use Adam on the manually computed local updates, not backprop through relaxation. Start with small blockwise statistics if elementwise moments do not fit; that is an approximation, not standard Adam. Full inherited FP32 Adam moments add **1.327GiB**, before temporaries. Moments, bias-correction step and schedule must save/restore with weights; the old eta cannot be copied numerically into Adam. No weight decay experiment is proposed by default.
- **What the user should see [Inference]:** preserve the newer recognizable words while producing more requested one-word answers and fewer irrelevant “The tore …” openings. A relevant answer such as “Wednesday” should replace the generic string when asked what follows Tuesday; this is an illustrative success criterion, not a forecast. Preventing a fresh collapse is valuable retention, but unchanged wrong text is not plateau recovery.
- **Risk:** less byte learning can merely make the plateau quieter. Adaptive normalization may amplify a biased/noisy direction. A global clip dominated by the largest matrix can starve small blocks.
- **Timing and rejection [Inference]:** transition warmup covers roughly the first15 minutes at the observed rate; assess the resulting text over the rest of the two-hour window. No rollback, bounded final updates and reduced byte-error drift are secondary checks. If text does not improve, halving byte updates may merely slow learning. Adafactor’s clipping thresholds and row/column-statistics ablations show that plausible normalization choices can fail almost completely; PCX also reports adverse AdamW width scaling. A21.3× coefficient is not evidence that a21.3× reduction is optimal. [L5–L7, P1, P5, O4–O6]

### 3. Replace the conditional squared-error factor with a consistently derived categorical local factor

- **Map:** `gpu/tensors.rs::{compute_byte_prediction_error_gpu,relax_byte_prediction_gpu,compute_batch_energy_gpu_tensor,update_weights_gpu_contrastive}`; `gpu/mod.rs::GpuPcn::settle_step`; `core::BytePredictionHead`; `universal_checkpoint.rs`’s saved byte-prediction metadata; `train_universal.rs::generator_heldout_evaluation` and the shared scorer path.
- **Change:** reuse the existing **W3 byte slab and bias**, but define a categorical predictive factor over the257 existing symbols. For logits `z=tanh(x2)W_byte+c`, supervised categorical energy is `λ[logsumexp(z)−z_target]`, with local logit residual `λ(softmax(z)−one_hot(target))`. Its adjacent hidden force and local weight derivatives can be computed analytically without reverse-mode training. This is the local factor’s formula, **not a complete substitute for the existing two-phase energy**: derive the free versus clamped state domains and every tied-weight contribution consistently, and use the same predictive distribution in heldout scoring and generation. Do not paste the residual into one kernel while retaining incompatible energies/readout elsewhere; do not add a parallel prose head.
- **Controls:** `--byte-prediction-precision` exists, but **no categorical-loss selector currently exists**. A new objective/version selector and preserved checkpoint bias/weights are required. Ramp the new contribution; do not reinterpret the saved bias as a log-frequency prior. CPU/GPU parity must cover the new factor; `OBJECTIVE-RESULT.md` explicitly warned that historical CPU probes ignored the conditional energy.
- **What the user should see [Literature → Inference]:** more complete valid words and, crucially, words that satisfy the request rather than unrelated lexical fragments. For “Join sun flower,” “sunflower” is success; “The formed flower …” is only surface improvement. Categorical local energies have direct PC-language evidence, but the gain in that paper is not a clean output-loss-only ablation.
- **Risk:** highest derivation/compatibility risk of these proposals; tied generative and predictive roles may conflict. Changing objective changes energy scales and cannot be judged by the old total-energy baseline. Preserve the previous checkpoint/binary as rollback evidence, not a silently replaced run.
- **Timing and rejection [Inference]:** separate any immediate decoding/readout change from learning across the following approximately1000 updates. Within two hours, look first for visibly more correct/semantically acceptable generated answers; normalized NLL, non-space/top-5 and retention support that result. Better NLL with the same irrelevant strings is not this user’s success. Do not promise coherent answers: PCX has a CIFAR10 example where MSE beats CE, and Laborieux’s large recovery came mainly from contrastive-estimator correction, not its subsequent CE/readout change. [P1, P2, P6, O3, O7]

### 4. Deterministically interleave selected source/response rows instead of long homogeneous blocks

- **Map:** `dataset_registry.rs::load_registry_stage`, `universal_corpus.rs::sequence_training_positions`, and `train_universal.rs::run`’s `group_examples` loop (`3591–3604`).
- **Change:** retain the same selected records, their response targets and all holdout exclusions; deterministically permute/interleave the inherited examples before batching, keeping their source IDs and deferred-credit metadata attached. Do **not** shuffle bytes within a context. Keep batch128, source quotas and loss unchanged. There is **no inspected shuffle/interleave CLI**; introduce an explicit, reproducible data-order control if implemented.
- **What the user should see [Inference]:** fewer swings between more word-like and more garbled answers when training moves among sources; newly learned task-appropriate responses should survive the next source block. This is an order-only trial, not a new-data intervention and not a claim that shuffling can create missing task knowledge.
- **Risk:** incorrect cursor accounting can skip/double-count training or break exact resume. Current stage-end commit semantics must remain correct, including rows whose credit is deferred. An apparent Dolly-only gain might sacrifice book continuation.
- **Timing and rejection [Inference]:** a changed sequence of updates begins immediately; a source-boundary benefit is observable within two hours only if relevant boundaries occur then. Compare actual answers on the same fixed prompts before/after such boundaries, with source-tagged diagnostics second. The existing rolling probe schedule alone may not repeat prompts soon enough; do not report stability merely because seven different questions look different. If matched prompt-fit does not improve, reject the causal hypothesis. Published curricula sometimes worsen LM loss or lose late gains; preserve sampling quotas/cursors rather than assuming any new order is beneficial. [L10, O11]

### 5. Preserve the original instruction in the existing conditioning coordinates

- **Map:** `universal.rs::{byte_scoring_input,CONDITION_INPUT_ROWS}`; `universal_corpus.rs::{sequence_prompt,prepared_response_byte_example}`; `train_universal.rs::{UniversalGpuScorer,run}` and the inherited update-scope call.
- **Change:** carry the canonical original instruction/conversation condition separately from the rolling generated suffix, during both prepared-response training and every decoding step. Use the existing request/full-state coordinates; gradually enable their contribution/plasticity without resetting any saved weight or adding a duplicate prose/code head. Keep future response bytes out of the condition. There is **no current flag that supplies this missing persistent input contract**.
- **What the user should see [Source → Inference]:** the same subject should produce the answer requested by its instruction—e.g. plural “child”→“children,” reversal “child”→“dlihc”—and longer answers should remain on the original question rather than converge to a common suffix. These are illustrative held-out behavioral contrasts, not instructions to train on dashboard tests. Full prompt availability alone will not teach either operation.
- **Risk:** existing condition rows were inactive/frozen on this route; abruptly enabling their stored values can perturb the inherited representation. Merely hashing a full prompt does not guarantee it can be learned. This is not a promise of conversational competence in two hours.
- **Timing and rejection [Inference]:** input distinguishability can be checked immediately without counting it as language progress. Within two hours, seek correct instruction-dependent generated content, not just different strings or first-byte scores. The47/47 initial subject-retention finding means this is a lower-confidence cure for the present short-answer failures; Lost in the Middle further shows that available context can still go unused. Keep byte retention checks, version changed input fingerprints, and compare the same records/targets. No promise of multi-turn competence in this window. [O10]

## A practical two-hour decision window—generated text first

**Measured timing / calculated projection:** post-second-rollback batch intervals had median6.183s and mean7.179s. At that workload, two elapsed hours correspond to approximately **1000 batches /128,000 supervised byte rows**, plus15–16 ordinary heldout evaluations. The actual prose-answer cadence is approximately15–16 minutes: **only7–8 fresh generated answers**, not all48 probes, in two elapsed hours. A complete rotation takes roughly12½ hours and can be longer with failures. Two hours of training-only GPU time need not equal two wall-clock hours.

**Inference/proposal, after one authorized candidate is implemented and verified:**

1. Preserve the latest exact state, recovery anchor, data cursors and new optimizer/schedule state. Keep the100-step profile, width and decoding policy unchanged unless the candidate explicitly changes the shared scorer. Capture immediate post-cutover outputs separately; do not count a readout/inference discontinuity as learning.
2. **Primary view: Expected vs actual.** Label each generated answer (a) correct/semantically acceptable, (b) relevant but wrong/incomplete, or (c) unrelated/garbled. Keep the exact text beside the expected answer. Accept reasonable equivalent phrasing; do not award factual credit merely for “The ” or punish a correct answer solely for harmless extra punctuation.
3. **Two-hour engineering target, not statistical proof:** move from all observed answers wrong to at least **two independently prompted, genuinely correct answers among the next roughly eight**, with less malformed text and no loss of existing behavior. A smaller relevant-but-wrong shift is partial prompt-fit progress. Higher dictionary coverage alone is lexical progress only. Eight changing prompts are too few for a broad capability claim; persistence needs repeated prompts and a wider held-out set afterward.
4. To establish same-prompt changes within two hours, a later authorized evaluation must reuse a small fixed, train-disjoint subset through the existing between-batch probe mechanism; the present48-question rotation will not provide that comparison by itself. Keep training active; no isolated GPU benchmark. Do not train on the diagnostic answers. This report submits no new probe or schedule change.
5. **Secondary diagnostics:** retain health gates, finiteness, local-force/update measures and the fixed512-window fingerprint. A sustained approximately3-point top-1 and2-point non-space gain would support improvement, but **is neither required nor sufficient for the user-visible claim**. Repeated use of the same512 windows is not independent replication; compare per-window changes and a second train-disjoint set before generalizing.
6. Read existing `portal/app.js::renderGenerator` fields for heldout, objective and safety context; no force/NLL/update display is claimed to exist today. Record Jev results for reference, without pretending intentionally untrained typed outputs are the measure of prose progress. Preserve the established shared prose/code route.

**Revised decision [Inference]:** first distinguish finite-inference/estimator problems from poorly balanced local updates while retaining data/instruction learning as live explanations. Then make one conservative, reversible change and judge its generated answers. There is no evidence-established reason to abandon this run, reduce width or100 steps, or switch to backprop; equally, there is no basis to promise that more data, a larger vocabulary, smaller eta, or a categorical factor will inevitably fix its text.
